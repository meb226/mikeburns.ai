Layer 01

The Knowledge Framework

Not a list of tactics. A structured understanding of how advocacy outcomes unfold, drawn from peer-reviewed research and practitioner experience that generic AI has never seen.

Consolidated from Real Expertise

We distilled 50 strategic principles from peer-reviewed research on lobbying effectiveness and interviews with senior practitioners. This is a decision framework that understands context.

The principles encode hard-won lessons: why 60% of lobbying campaigns fail to change policy, how defenders hold structural advantages, when coalition diversity matters more than size, and why resources alone explain less than 5% of success.

Each principle includes conditional logic. Status quo defense requires different positioning than offensive campaigns. High-salience issues demand different tool selection than quiet regulatory fights. The framework adapts recommendations to your prospect's specific situation.

Most importantly, these principles stay invisible in your output. The memo doesn't cite research or drop academic jargon. It applies the insights naturally, the way a seasoned BD partner would: informed by deep knowledge, but speaking the language of your prospect.

The difference: Generic AI summarizes what lobbying is. PitchSource knows when to recommend grasstops mobilization versus quiet inside-game engagement, when to emphasize coalition breadth versus depth, and how to position defensive versus offensive campaigns.

Why Multi-Stage Matters

Single-pass generation optimizes for completeness. Multi-stage refinement optimizes for persuasion. The critique stage asks questions your prospect will actually ask.

"Do they actually have experience in my sector?" "Why should I believe they can deliver on this timeline?" "What makes them different from my current firm?"

Three Generation Modes

Choose your workflow based on time and quality needs.

  • Draft: Single-stage generation. Fastest output.
  • Standard: Full 4-stage workflow in background.
  • Detailed: Watch each stage stream. Intervene if needed.

The result: Memos that have already been stress-tested against the hardest questions. When you walk into the pitch, you've already heard the objections and addressed them.
